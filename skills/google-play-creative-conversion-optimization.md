# Google Play Creative Conversion Optimization

## Purpose

Improve Google Play listing conversion with truthful, form-factor-specific icons, screenshots, feature graphics, and preview videos that demonstrate the product's value quickly.

## When To Use

Use when auditing or producing a creative set, screenshot storyboard, feature graphic brief, app icon brief, or store-listing experiment.

## Inputs Needed

- Current listing assets by locale and form factor.
- Current app UI and release version.
- Primary user job and differentiators.
- Query and intent map.
- Play Console conversion data by segment if approved.
- Current official asset requirements.
- Brand constraints.
- Competitor creative observations for pattern analysis only.

## Creative Principles

1. Demonstrate the real product.
2. Communicate one primary promise per asset.
3. Make the first impression understandable at small size.
4. Keep visual identity consistent across icon, listing, app, website, and videos.
5. Use screenshots to reduce uncertainty, not to create unsupported fantasy.
6. Design separately for phone, tablet, Wear OS, TV, Auto, and XR requirements.
7. Localize promotional text.
8. Avoid tiny text, decorative clutter, stale device frames, fake notifications, rankings, awards, pricing claims, or unsupported features.

## Workflow

### 1. Diagnose the Current Funnel

For each asset, record:

| Asset | Surface | Primary Promise | Product Evidence | Readability | Policy Risk | Conversion Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|

Common issues:

- Icon does not communicate category or is unreadable at small size.
- First screenshot starts with settings rather than value.
- Screenshot captions repeat instead of advancing a story.
- Feature graphic is generic branding with no product context.
- Preview video delays actual UI.
- Wear OS screenshots contain device frames or marketing overlays where current rules prohibit them.
- Assets show old UI or unsupported features.
- Phone and watch assets tell conflicting stories.

### 2. Design the Screenshot Narrative

Recommended sequence:

1. Primary outcome.
2. Fastest core workflow.
3. Strong differentiator.
4. Secondary high-intent feature.
5. Trust, privacy, offline, compatibility, or customization proof.
6. Advanced workflow or companion experience.
7. Supportive ecosystem feature such as Tile, widget, complication, shortcut, or integration.
8. Final proof or breadth, not a generic call to action.

Use fewer positions when the product is simple. Do not invent breadth.

For each screenshot specify:

- Exact screen or state.
- User promise.
- Evidence visible in UI.
- Optional concise caption.
- Crop and safe-area notes.
- Locale and form factor.
- Capture instructions.
- Policy checks.

### 3. Improve the Icon

Evaluate:

- Small-size silhouette.
- Category recognizability.
- Distinctiveness from direct competitors.
- Contrast across Play surfaces.
- Adaptive-icon safety where applicable.
- Consistency with launcher and product identity.
- Absence of misleading badges, text, rankings, store marks, or copied branding.

Create a test hypothesis rather than changing the icon casually, because icon changes affect brand recognition.

### 4. Improve the Feature Graphic

- Verify current dimensions and format.
- Center important elements for variable crops.
- Extend the app identity without duplicating the icon.
- Communicate the product experience or value proposition.
- Keep text minimal and readable.
- Avoid unsupported claims, rankings, awards, pricing, store badges, device obsolescence, and dense UI.

### 5. Improve Preview Video

- Show real product value immediately.
- Prioritize actual UI.
- Design for muted autoplay.
- Keep pacing concise.
- Add accurate captions where appropriate.
- Ensure the video is embeddable and compliant with current Play requirements.
- Use the correct form-factor story.

### 6. Create Experiment Variants

Each variant must change one primary hypothesis where practical:

```text
icon recognition
first-screenshot promise
screenshot order
caption framing
feature emphasis
feature graphic value proposition
preview-video opening
localized creative
```

Keep all other material variables stable or document the confound.

## Wear OS Requirements

When Wear OS is in scope:

- Verify current Wear OS screenshot rules before delivery.
- Capture current watch UI only.
- Use required aspect ratio and resolution.
- Do not add device frames, backgrounds, overlays, or marketing text when prohibited.
- Include Tile functionality when it is a meaningful shipped feature and current guidance recommends it.
- Check round-screen clipping, large font, black backgrounds, rotary behavior, and supported watch sizes separately from listing creative.
- Keep companion-phone and watch screenshots clearly distinguished.

## Quality Bar

- The first asset communicates the main job quickly.
- Every promise has visible product evidence.
- Form-factor rules are verified.
- The storyboard is capture-ready.
- Experiments isolate a useful hypothesis.
