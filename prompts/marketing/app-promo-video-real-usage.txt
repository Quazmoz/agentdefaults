PHASE 3 — RECORD BELIEVABLE INTERACTIONS
Record the actual display while operating the running app. On Android, use supported tools such as adb screenrecord or an emulator recording facility; use screencap for inspection and poster sources. Confirm capture includes the soft keyboard and real state changes. Keep raw takes under OUTPUT_DIR/captures/raw/ and reusable capture scripts under OUTPUT_DIR/capture/.

Interaction rules:
- Show the entire causal sequence: control selected → input or gesture → visible app response → meaningful result. Record at least one core flow continuously before editing it.
- Text entry must look like text entry: focus the real field, show the real keyboard, enter characters progressively at a readable human pace, pause naturally, then use the actual next/done/save control. A pasted whole string appearing instantly does not satisfy the typing requirement. As a starting point, use about 4–8 characters per second and brief pauses between fields; adapt to the content and available time.
- Use real keyboard taps or supported per-character input that produces actual field changes. Check spaces, punctuation, capitalization, and Unicode in the rendered field; an automation tool accepting the string does not prove it entered correctly. Do not animate a text overlay over an empty field or synthesize keyboard/key states.
- Tap the true hit targets. Show a restrained touch indicator only for an action that was actually performed, using recorded input coordinates and timestamps or supported system touch visualization. No desktop cursor pretending to be a finger, random tap rings, or gestures that precede their real actions.
- Use real swipes to scroll content and real controls to navigate. Let natural inertial movement, keyboard transitions, selection states, loading, and confirmation appear. Do not animate a screenshot to fake scrolling or make a tap ring stand in for a real click.
- After each important action, allow enough time to read the changed state. Hold the main result for about 1–2 seconds. Keep deliberate pauses brief; avoid frantic tapping, instant teleporting between screens, and a full keyboard covering the payoff.
- Show correction or back-navigation only when useful to the story. Do not manufacture errors, notifications, permissions, or features just to add motion.
- Preserve continuity: the value typed is the value submitted and later shown; selection, ordering, theme, and scroll position match across cuts. Rehearse/reset before another take rather than stitching incompatible states.

Use a few short takes with clear jobs:
- Core workflow: a continuous input-to-result take, usually 8–15 seconds of usable footage.
- Supporting interactions: one or two focused 3–5-second takes when available, such as searching for the item just created, changing a real option, opening details, or interacting with a verified widget/Tile.
- Hero/result: a clean live view of the real final state for the ending and poster.

Capture at a resolution that keeps field text legible in the final phone framing. Inspect each take before editing. If typing, touches, text, keyboard, or outcome are missing or incorrect, fix the capture rather than hiding the problem in a transition. Use at most three takes per workflow, with a specific correction between attempts.

In CAPTURE.md, keep the replay command, starting-state/reset instructions, tool versions, raw clip metadata, and an action table: action/typed value → control or coordinates → source timestamp → expected state → observed state. Save before/after evidence frames for core actions. Record only non-sensitive metadata. Preserve raw footage; cuts and retiming belong in the edit.

PHASE 4 — EDIT A PROMO THAT TEACHES THE WORKFLOW
Use the real recording as the animated screen inside a correctly proportioned generic phone or watch. Favor a front-facing or gently angled phone during typing and important taps, large enough to read both field content and the result. Keep decorative movement restrained while the viewer needs to follow an action.

Default story (adjust within the duration and capture-coverage requirements):
- 0–3 s: open on a live action or visible payoff with a short problem/benefit hook. No black fade or logo-only intro.
- 3–18 s: establish the app category by about 4 s, then show the core workflow as "Enter → Choose → Save" or the app's real equivalent. Captions explain the user's intent while the footage proves the steps.
- 18–27 s: show useful supporting interactions and the resulting benefit. Use fewer features if that makes the core flow easier to follow.
- 27 s–end: show the actual result, exact app icon/name, and verified or neutral CTA. Resolve motion cleanly; loop when it fits naturally.

Editing rules:
- Use continuous captured motion during important interactions. Cut repetitive setup and waiting; preserve the initiating action and its consequence. Keep typing near real time. If long waits are removed, do not imply an unsupported speed claim.
- Keep an edit map from each final scene to its raw clip and source time range. If footage is retimed, retime touch indicators and callouts with it. Callouts must remain anchored to the real controls through cropping, device transforms, and camera moves.
- Preserve the capture's aspect ratio and real content. Scale/crop/mask without stretching, relabeling controls, painting in values, or generating replacement UI. Do not use AI video interpolation or generation to invent intermediate app states or finger actions.
- Any touch/caption/step annotation must be visibly editorial and must not obscure its control or look like app UI. A composited hand is optional only if it accurately follows recorded actions and leaves the important UI visible; it is unnecessary if simple touch visualization is clearer.
- For Wear OS, use a round watch mask at true display proportions, never a phone frame. Show phone/watch interaction only after recording and verifying the real integration; no invented sync.
- Match the actual brand. Use a clean studio background, subtle accent lighting, and minimal device motion. Avoid repeated spins, excessive zooms, particle effects, and transitions that hide the action.
- Keep each headline to 2–6 words and supporting copy to one short line. At 1080 px width, aim for headlines of at least 72 px and supporting text of at least 44 px, with contrast of at least 4.5:1. Allow at least 0.5 s plus 0.3 s per word. The result must be understandable muted.
- Use conservative overlay margins at 1080×1920: key copy, icon, controls being used, and results inside x 90–930 and y 220–1480. Background and device edges may extend beyond. Check the actual phone/keyboard composition against these bounds.

Choose the simplest reproducible render pipeline that works with available tools. FFmpeg, Remotion, or another local editor/render engine is acceptable; do not build a reusable framework or elaborate 3D system unless it materially improves this video. Pin added dependencies with a lockfile, record versions/licenses, and keep app identity, footage paths, scene timing, and copy easy to change. Ignore dependencies, caches, and intermediates in OUTPUT_DIR/.gitignore.

Audio is optional. Use only licensed repository audio or locally generated/procedural audio whose rights can be recorded in ASSETS.md. Keep effects sparse and tied to real actions, music under the UI story, and audio around −14 LUFS integrated with a true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. A polished silent video is acceptable; disclose it. Do not add audio that suggests an app feedback sound or feature it does not have.
