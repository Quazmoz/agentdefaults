3A. FORM-FACTOR COVERAGE (mandatory when both apps are present)
======================================================================
Presence (decide in Phase 1.1 with file:line evidence, in this repository and any COMPANION_REPO):
- An Android phone app is present when an application module without the watch feature has a launcher activity and real user features. A phone module that only installs or sets up the watch app is not a phone app; record the proof.
- A Wear OS app is present when an application module declares <uses-feature android:name="android.hardware.type.watch"> and ships at least one user-facing surface: an activity, a tile (TileService), a complication data source, a watch face, or an ongoing activity. A headless module (Data Layer listener only) is not a Wear OS app.
- A module that is a sample, test, benchmark, or excluded from release builds (settings.gradle, release CI, disabled manifest entry) does not count. Record each excluded module with the evidence; never exclude one to make the film easier.
- Both present means mode PAIR_INDEPENDENT or PAIR_CONNECTED. In those modes every rule below is required, not preferred.

Coverage, when both are present:
1. Both apps appear with their own real UI: phone screens on the phone, watch screens on the round watch, each also lifted out of its own device. A watch component lifted out keeps its round clip, curved text, and real layout.
2. Each form factor is the focal subject for at least 25% of the running time (at least 7.5 s of a 30 s film), counted from the shot list and confirmed on the render. A device that is only in frame, small or out of focus, does not count toward its share.
3. Each form factor performs at least one real moment from its own moments inventory: an action and its visible result, not a static screen. On the watch, use the input the app really implements (tap, rotary or crown scroll, tile tap, complication tap). Prefer the watch's signature surface: its tile or complication when that is the primary entry point, otherwise its main screen.
4. At least one shared shot shows both devices together at believable relative scale (a watch display is roughly a quarter of the phone's height), in the Hook, the Close, or both. Bring one forward in depth instead of scaling the other up.
5. The film opens with both form factors established by the end of the Hook or the first phone/watch shot, so a muted viewer knows within 8 s that the app runs on phone and watch.
6. The close resolves from a two-device frame into the icon lockup. The poster shows both devices or a lifted component from each.

Links between devices:
- PAIR_INDEPENDENT: no transition carries a UI element, value, or user data from one device to the other. Move between devices with a camera travel, a match on a neutral shape, or a match on the brand color only. Never show identical user data on both screens at the same time, and no super or tagline implies they work together ("syncs", "together", "on your wrist and your phone").
- PAIR_CONNECTED: shared-element handoffs between devices may travel only in the code-proven direction, carrying only the proven payload, matching the captured runtime effect. Never state or count a sync latency you did not measure.

If one form factor cannot be built, run, or captured, keep it in the film: rebuild it from source with fidelity marked PARTIAL against repository screenshots, and report the blocker. If it cannot be rebuilt faithfully either, finish everything else and report status partial. Never drop a present form factor to reach a finished status.

======================================================================
4. THE STYLE (what makes it read as an Apple app ad)
======================================================================
Learn these principles. Never copy Apple's trade dress, typefaces (SF Pro is licensed only for mockups of Apple-platform UI), music, footage, or tagline wording, and never draw Apple hardware.

1. The UI is the cast. Most of the frame is the app's own components (cards, rows, chips, badges, inputs, sheets) shown large and crisp, often lifted out of the device and floating on the field. The device appears to set context, then the camera moves into a component and the component steps out of the screen. Plan for at least 60% of the running time to be UI-dominant.
2. One idea per shot, about 1.5–3 s each, one focal subject on a quiet field. A shot shows a single action and its result: a card picks itself, text is typed, a reaction pops, a row climbs a rank.
3. Demonstrate, don't describe. The story is told by things happening in the UI: typing with a caret in the app's real input, a tap that lands and resolves, a counter that ticks, a list that reorders. Words on screen come mostly from inside the app.
4. Supers are rare and short. At most four supers in the whole film, five words or fewer each, besides the end card. Many great spots have none until the wordmark. A super never repeats what the picture already shows.
5. Continuous motion, no hard cuts. Each shot hands off through a shared element: the card becomes the screen, the screen zooms into a row, the row becomes the next card, the cards become a wall. Something is always gently moving, including a slow camera drift during holds.
6. Tactile depth. Floating components cast soft, believable shadows, tilt in shallow 3D, and settle with springs. Depth comes from layering and parallax, not from glow, bokeh, gradients behind everything, or particles.
7. One breadth moment. Once in the film, show the scale of the app: every template, item, or category as a wall, grid, or slowly turning cylinder of real cards, each one a faithful rebuild. Use only items that really exist in the shipped app, and count them.
8. One payoff hold. The strongest result (completion, reward, rank, streak, or saved outcome) holds for 1.5–2.5 s with the music landing on it.
9. Warm restraint. One soft, light, slightly warm field (default #F2F0EA, or a tone taken from the brand) with near-black type. Saturated color lives only on the app's UI, the icon, and the devices.
10. Close on identity. Icon (any real alternate icons the app ships may cycle on the beat), then the wordmark or name, then a tagline typed or revealed under it, of five words or fewer and traced to the PROPOSITION. Hold the end card at least 2 s.

PHASE 3 — LOOK, DEVICES, AND MOTION
======================================================================
- Type: supers semibold, tracking about −0.03 em, at least 56 px at 1080 px width (64 px at 1920 width), inside the title-safe area (5% margins; for 9:16 also keep clear of typical platform overlay zones). Contrast at least 4.5:1.
- In-UI text the viewer must read renders at least 28 px tall on the output frame; push the camera in or lift the component out until it does. A watch screen the viewer reads is usually at least 620 px in diameter on a 1080 px-wide frame.
- Reading time: hold any text the viewer must read for at least 0.3 s per word plus 0.5 s, fully still.
- Devices (Three.js, or CSS 3D for simple shots): a generic phone with no OEM logos and the correct corner radius and punch-hole; a generic round watch with a crown at 3 o'clock, a neutral strap, no OEM silhouette, and a screen that is a true circle clipped like the real round display. Believable relative scale in shared shots.
- Watch UI faithfulness: match the app's Wear Compose Material 2 or Material 3 stack as source shows it: TimeText curved along the top edge, scaling lists that shrink and fade toward the edges, round-safe padding, and tile and complication layouts from their ProtoLayout or complication code. Complications appear on a generic, fictional watch face you design, never a real OEM or third-party face.
- Lifted components: rendered from the same HTML as the in-screen version so the handoff is pixel-continuous; soft layered shadows; shallow 3D tilt (≤12°); springs settle with little or no overshoot.
- Typing: characters appear at a human rhythm (about 8–12 per second with small seeded variation), with a blinking caret on the beat, in the app's real input field and font.
- Taps: a touch indicator on the true hit target with a short press state (a soft fingertip on the phone, a thin ring on the watch so it does not hide the small screen); crown or rotary scrolls show the crown turning in step with the list; the UI responds on the next beat. Inside the device screen, the UI moves only the way the real app moves (its own navigation, list, and dialog transitions).
- Easing roles: expo-out for entrances, expo-in for exits, expo-in-out for travel and camera; a few named springs (pop, snappy, ui, move) defined once in config.
- Purity: every frame is a pure function render(t). No wall-clock timers, requestAnimationFrame state, unseeded randomness, or free-running CSS animations. Wait for fonts, images, and WebGL before capture.
- Flash safety (WCAG 2.3.1): never more than 3 flashes in any 1 s window, and no large saturated-red flashes, including icon cycles and beat-synced cuts.

GATE G3: stills of the hook, one lifted component per present app, the breadth moment, and the end card sit next to their references in qc/fidelity/, with layout, text, color, and order differences fixed or listed.
