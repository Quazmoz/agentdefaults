3. TRUTH RULES FOR A CODE-BUILT FILM
======================================================================
Every app screen is rebuilt in HTML/CSS so it can animate perfectly. That only works if the rebuild is faithful.

Screens and flows
- Rebuild only screens and states that exist and are reachable in the shipped variant. Take layout, copy, colors, fonts, icons, and spacing from source: Compose or XML layouts, strings.xml, theme files, drawables, ProtoLayout tile code, Watch Face Format XML.
- Copy UI text from strings.xml exactly. Never paraphrase a label.
- Animated flows follow the real order of actions and the real results. A rebuild may simplify scrollable content. It may not add a control, value, feature, or outcome the app does not have.
- Use the device that owns each screen. A watch screen never appears on the phone, and a phone screen never appears on the watch.

Claims
- Every spoken or on-screen claim traces to a VERIFIED entry in BRIEF.md: file:line, plus runtime proof where the claim is about behavior.
- Prove absence claims ("works offline", "no account", "no ads") against the manifests, every module's dependencies (watch and phone), and the network code.
- Use exact behavior. Never "secure", "private", "100% safe", "fastest", "seamless", or "instant".
- Features behind a purchase or entitlement appear with the app's own lock or Pro affordance, or the narration says they are paid. Never imply a paid feature is free.

Watch and cross-device claims (these need runtime proof, not just code)
- "Syncs", "on your phone and your watch", "send to watch", "control from your wrist", or any shown data moving between devices: requires PAIR_CONNECTED (Phase 1) and a captured runtime effect on both emulators.
- "Works without your phone": requires com.google.android.wearable.standalone set to true in the watch manifest AND a run of the claimed workflow on an unpaired watch emulator.
- Tiles, complications, ongoing activities, rotary input, and ambient mode appear only if the code implements them, and only as the code renders them.
- A shared element may travel between devices only in the code-proven direction (phone → watch, watch → phone, or both), carrying only the proven payload. Never state or count a sync latency you did not measure.

Fictional world
- When the story shows other apps or people (the "old way", contacts, content inside the app), make them fictional: invented names, generic layouts, no real brand names, logos, trade dress, or real people. Draw demo images as vector or CSS art and never make them look like a competitor's product.
- Complications are shown on a generic, fictional watch face that you design, never on a real OEM or third-party face.

4. CRAFT PRINCIPLES
======================================================================
These principles come from Apple's app and device advertising: the "Privacy. That's iPhone." and "Relax, it's iPhone." campaigns, the iPhone + Apple Watch "Big Fish" spot, the 2015 Apple Watch feature spots, and Apple's App Store app preview guidance. Learn from the principles. Never copy Apple's trade dress, typefaces (SF Pro is licensed only for mockups of Apple-platform UI), music, footage, or tagline wording.

1. One idea. The film proves a single promise, the PROPOSITION, written as one sentence of 12 words or fewer. Every scene makes it more believable or more specific. A scene that only adds a feature is cut or folded into Breadth.
2. The viewer is the hero and the app is the guide. Narrate the viewer's moment ("When your hands are full…"), not the app's feature list. Features enter as the answer to that moment.
3. Context makes the feature necessary. Stage the moment in which the feature is obviously the right tool. In pair modes, also stage the moment in which the wrist is obviously the right device: hands busy, phone across the room, a glance mid-task. Build the moment from fictional illustrated surroundings, never real people, and never let it imply behavior the app lacks.
4. Concrete metaphors for abstract claims. An abstract VERIFIED claim (offline, no account, data stays on the device) becomes an everyday image in motion, such as a blind closing or a door shutting. The metaphor must not overclaim: a shutting door may illustrate "never leaves this device" only if that claim is VERIFIED, and it never stands in for "encrypted" or "secure". Check how a stranger could misread it. Images of destroying or mocking things people value read as hostile even when they are meant to be clever.
5. Restraint. One idea per shot: one focal subject and one message, with generous negative space. A headline with its chip, or a cloud of items moving as one group, counts as one message. Saturated color lives only on the devices and the app's UI. Transitions are invisible on purpose. Sound effects are sparse and quiet, and the music's tempo matches the edit.
6. Let one moment breathe. At the payoff of the strongest workflow, hold for 3–5 s with no narration and let the picture and music land it.
7. Muted first. Feeds and store pages autoplay without sound. The first 3 s must hook on picture and text alone, and the whole story must survive with the sound off. Headlines and chips carry the claims; the narration deepens them.
8. Honest motion. Motion outside the device screen can be as expressive as you like. Inside the screen, the UI moves only the way the real app moves. Never animate a transition, gesture, or effect the app lacks. Prefer the UI at native proportions over a zoom so deep that it reads as a different layout.
9. A lockup that repeats. End on a short benefit line plus the name, built to work as a reusable campaign line (pattern: "<Benefit>. <App name>."). In pair modes, the lockup names both surfaces the way the film proved them, for example "<App name> for phone and Wear OS".
10. Disclose and date-proof. Say or show when a feature needs a purchase, a subscription, or a sign-in. Include no prices and no dated or seasonal references ("new this fall", a year) in the film, the thumbnail, or COPY.md.

PHASE 3 — LOOK, DEVICES, AND MOTION SYSTEM
======================================================================
Look
- One light editorial field (default white, near-black type #111). The devices and the app's own UI are the only dark or saturated objects, unless the brand clearly calls for a dark world. One accent color from the brand.
- App theme: if the phone app supports light and dark themes, use one (default: the app's default) for the whole film. Watch UI keeps its own background.
- One idea per shot (principle 5): one focal subject and one message (a headline with its chip counts as one), with the rest of the frame left empty. Viewer-moment scenes may use warmer illustrated surroundings, then return to the plain field for the next product beat.
- Typography: the app's font if its license covers video and advertising use (a font licensed for embedding in the app often does not), otherwise Inter or a similar licensed neo-grotesk. Headlines semibold, tracking about −0.035 em at display sizes, text-rendering: geometricPrecision. Headlines at least 64 px at 1080p and supporting text at least 32 px, with contrast of at least 4.5:1, inside the title-safe area (5% margin on each side).

Devices (Three.js, with the live HTML screen mapped on via CSS3DRenderer or an HTML-to-texture path, plus a subtle glass/reflection layer above the screen)
- Phone: generic hardware with the correct corner radius and punch-hole, and no OEM logos.
- Watch: a generic round case with a crown at 3 o'clock and a neutral strap. The screen is a true circle at the AVD's display proportions, clipped exactly like the real round display. No OEM silhouettes (no domed pebble, no rotating numbered bezel) and no logos. Record any model's license.
- Shared shots: keep believable relative scale; a watch display is roughly a quarter of the phone's height. Bring the hero device forward in depth instead of scaling the other one up. Never stage both at a matched size as if they were the same object.
- Legibility: any in-device text the viewer must read renders at a font size of at least 28 px at 1080p. Watch screens the viewer reads are usually at least 620 px in diameter on screen. If you cannot reach that, push the camera in or repeat the key value as an on-canvas chip.

Watch UI faithfulness (match the app's Material 2 or Material 3 stack)
- Curved TimeText at the top if the app uses it, a scroll or position indicator that appears while scrolling, list edge scaling exactly as ScalingLazyColumn or TransformingLazyColumn renders it, swipe-to-dismiss revealing the real previous screen, and the app's own (usually black) backgrounds.
- The crown turns only on screens that handle rotary input in code, and the content scrolls with it at a plausible ratio.
- Tiles and complications are rebuilt from their ProtoLayout or complication data code. Show ambient mode only if it is implemented, using its real reduced layout.

Motion rules
- Every frame is a pure function render(t): any frame renders on its own, out of order, identically every time. No wall-clock timers, no requestAnimationFrame-driven state, no unseeded randomness, and no free-running CSS transitions or animations. Drive everything from t, seed every random source, and wait for fonts, images, and WebGL before capture.
- One role per easing curve: expo-out for entrances, expo-in or back-in for exits, expo-in-out for travel and camera. UI elements use a few named springs (pop, snappy, ui, move), defined once in config.
- Never hard-cut. Each scene hands off by morphing a shared element: the motif becomes an input, the input becomes the device, the device becomes cards, the cards become the cloud. Cross-device morphs follow the proven data direction only.
- Honest motion (principle 8): morphs and camera moves happen outside the device screen. Inside it, screen changes use the app's real transitions (navigation, list, and dialog animations taken from source, or the platform defaults when the source sets none).
- Each scene gets a subtle camera breath (scale 1 → 1.03 → 1) so holds never freeze.
- Text reveals word by word on the voice's word clock: each word rises a few pixels, unblurs, and fades in, then the line holds still and readable.
- Taps: a touch indicator lands on the true hit target of the rebuilt control, with a short press state, and the UI responds on the next beat. On the phone it is a soft fingertip; on the watch it is a thin ring, so it does not hide the small screen. Pace every tap so a first-time viewer can follow it. Calm beats fast.
- No glow, bokeh, or particle spam in diagrams.
- Flash safety (WCAG 2.3.1): never more than 3 flashes in any 1 s window, and no large saturated-red flashes. This includes the outro burst and beat-synced cards.

GATE G3 (Look): stills of one phone screen and one watch screen (as the mode requires) sit next to their references in qc/fidelity/, with differences in layout, text, color, and order fixed or listed.

PHASE 4 — VOICE, MUSIC, SOUND, AND MIX
======================================================================
Before using any model, verify that its current license permits commercial advertising use. Record every tool and model with its version and license in ASSETS.md. Cache each voice clip and music render by a hash of its inputs (model, voice, settings, text, seed) so re-renders are fast and identical.

Voiceover (the goal is a voice a first-time viewer takes for a calm human narrator, not a TTS demo)
- Model shortlist. Use a local open-weight model whose weights AND voices permit commercial advertising use. Check the current license text on each model card, because these change. As of late 2026, likely candidates are:
  - Chatterbox (MIT; Turbo or Multilingual): expressive, with exaggeration and cfg_weight controls. Every output carries an imperceptible PerTh watermark; record it in ASSETS.md.
  - Qwen3-TTS (Apache 2.0): preset speakers, plus plain-language direction of tone and pacing.
  - Kokoro-82M (Apache 2.0): fast and clean, but has no expressive controls. Use it as the baseline to beat.
  - Orpheus 3B: only after confirming that Meta's Llama license terms are met, and record how.
  - Dia (Apache 2.0): only for a second speaker.
- Do not use these, because their weights, voices, or outputs are non-commercial or research-only: XTTS v2, F5-TTS weights, Fish Audio S2, Breeze TTS, VibeVoice, IndexTTS without a bilibili agreement, Voxtral reference voices, or any Piper voice whose dataset license is not commercial.
- Voice identity: use a model's built-in preset voice. Clone only from a reference recording I supply and state I have consent for. Never clone a real person from the internet, a dataset speaker, or a celebrity sound-alike.
- Voice brief: one paragraph in BRIEF.md. For example: "warm, unhurried, conversational, mid-low pitch, a slight smile, explaining something to a friend across the table, never announcing or selling." Use it to choose the preset, as the direction prompt for models that accept one, and as the listening rubric.
- Bake-off before full synthesis: render the same three test lines (the opening line, the line with the app name, and the pivot question) with each shortlisted model and voice that runs on this machine, using 2–3 settings each. Give the files neutral names, compare them blind, pick one, and record why.
- Synthesize by thought, not by line. One clip per beat or paragraph (2–4 script lines) gives the model context, so intonation rises and falls across sentences the way a person's does. Line-by-line synthesis produces list-reading cadence and is not allowed except to fix a single word. Cache each chunk by its input hash.
- Takes: render 3–5 takes per chunk with different seeds or temperatures, then score each take:
  (a) faster-whisper transcript against the script: every content word and the app name are correct;
  (b) speaking rate of 2.3–2.6 words per second;
  (c) pitch movement: reject takes whose F0 contour is markedly flatter than the other takes (monotone) or that jumps erratically;
  (d) no artifacts: no metallic or robotic tone, clipped word starts, mid-word cuts, stutters, or internal silence over 0.8 s.
  (e) Optionally, an automatic naturalness predictor (a MOS model) if its license permits.
  Keep the best take per chunk, and write the scores to qc/reports/voice.md.
- Listening: an agent may not be able to hear audio. Never claim you listened unless you actually did. If you cannot listen, select takes on the measured scores, mark naturalness as UNVERIFIED, and, if I am available, give me the two best takes of each chunk to choose between. A take that fails (a) or (d) after 5 tries means the line itself is the problem: rewrite it for the ear.
- Pronunciation: fixes go into the TTS input only, as phonetic respelling or phoneme markup. Captions and on-screen text keep the real spelling, and the alignment step maps respelled tokens back.
- Light, transparent processing only:
  - a high-pass filter around 70–80 Hz and a gentle de-esser;
  - light compression (about 2:1, a few dB of gain reduction);
  - an optional, barely audible short room ambience so the voice does not sound sterile;
  - each chunk matched to the same loudness before the mix.
  Never pitch-shift. Time-stretch by no more than ±4%, using a formant-preserving method.
- Edges and pauses: keep natural breaths. Trim silence with 40–80 ms of pre-roll and 10 ms fades, so word starts are never clipped. Vary the gaps by meaning: 0.25–0.4 s inside a thought, 0.6–1.0 s at a beat or scene change, plus the narration-free hold. Move the picture to fit the voice; never stretch the voice to hit a cut.

Word clock
- Transcribe each chosen take with faster-whisper (medium.en or better, word timestamps) and align it to the script tokens. A mismatched word means a misread line: fix the take, not the timing. Line boundaries inside a chunk come from the alignment.
- Build data/wordclock.json, mapping W(line, word) to seconds on the film timeline, and drive every text reveal, chip, tap, and scene event from it. Moving a line moves everything tied to it.
- Export .srt and .vtt captions from the same word clock.

Music
- Compose the score locally in code (Python/NumPy synthesis, or a free local synth already on the machine), seeded and regenerable from a script. Make 2–3 candidates per part: a calm, spacious opening for the old way, and a confident, warmer groove from "Meet the app" on (for example deep, clean electronic house). Do not imitate a named artist or a recognizable melody. Write real harmony, groove, phrasing, and variation, not a drone or a click track.
- Pick the best candidates and record why in BRIEF.md. Keep the rest in audio/candidates/ with a one-line command to swap each one in.
- Join the parts at a phrase boundary with an intentional transition, and give the film a real ending with a tail.

Beat grid
- Analyze the final music with librosa or similar: tempo, beats, downbeats, section changes, and key. Check the results against the waveform rather than trusting the intended BPM. Write data/beatgrid.json.
- Put cards, cloud entrances, major transitions, and the outro finale on the beat grid. Taps and text stay on the word clock; arrange around them rather than forcing them onto beats.

Sound design (synthesized in code, or CC0 libraries such as Kenney UI sounds, with licenses recorded)
- A deep, soft keyboard thock for typing; soft tonal notes (marimba-like) tuned to the music's key for cards and chips; light taps for phone touches; a smaller, higher tick for watch touches and crown detents; and a sub hit with a chord bloom in key for the outro. Keep transients sparse and clean, with no harsh whooshes.

Mix
- Duck the music by about 6 dB under the voice, with raised-cosine ramps of 150–250 ms. The voice must be clearly intelligible on laptop speakers.
- Master to about −14 LUFS integrated with true peak at or below −1 dBTP. Measure both the WAV master and the encoded AAC.
- Deliver a 48 kHz stereo WAV master plus voice, music, and SFX stems.

GATE G4 (Audio): the bake-off and take scores are recorded, every chunk is aligned with no content-word mismatches, listening is reported as done (by you or by me) or as UNVERIFIED, the music is chosen and analyzed, and loudness and true peak are measured and within target.

Render

Take one capture per output frame at 60 fps with no supersampling, split by frame range across parallel workers (default 4; match the machine).
